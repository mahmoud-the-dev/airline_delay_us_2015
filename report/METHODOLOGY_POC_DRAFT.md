# 4. Methodology

### a. Data cleaning and preparation

The working file is `raw/Flight_Delays_Cleaned.csv`, 50,000 rows from the 2015 BTS on-time extract in the Kaggle 50k pack. `src/clean.py` writes `clean/flights.parquet`, the dashboard never opens the CSV, and the grain stays one row per scheduled flight.

Arrival delay is blank on every cancelled flight and every diverted flight, 794 and 121 rows, because those flights never completed a scheduled arrival. Clock fields that never happened are blank in the same rows, and we did not impute them, because filling arrival delay would invent a landing time. `OPERATED` is false for cancelled and diverted rows, so `DELAYED` cannot treat a blank `ARRIVAL_DELAY` as late. On operated rows `ARRIVAL_DELAY` has no missing values, the Kaggle pack already filled cause-minute columns with zeros, and we left the remaining columns unchanged.

We called `drop_duplicates` and the row count stayed 50,000, because the extract had no duplicate scheduled-flight rows to remove.

`clean.py` does not recast columns, and pandas infers types from the CSV, so `CANCELLED` and `DIVERTED` come in as booleans, delay minutes as floats, and calendar fields as integers, which parquet then stores. October 2015 stores `ORIGIN_AIRPORT` and `DESTINATION_AIRPORT` as 5-digit BTS airport IDs (4,228 rows); every other month already uses 3-letter IATA. `clean.py` leaves 3-letter codes unchanged and replaces 5-digit IDs from the first sheet of `raw/lookups/BTS_2015_Airport_ID_IATA.xlsx` (322 ID→IATA pairs reconstructed for 2015 from the BTS 2021 historical archive, not a 2015-published lookup). In this extract every October ID matched, so leftover count is 0. If an ID had no match the cell would stay numeric; airport charts would drop those rows only, via `ORIGIN_IS_IATA` / `DEST_IS_IATA`, and would not drop the rest of October.

We added `OPERATED` as not cancelled and not diverted, `DELAYED` as operated and `ARRIVAL_DELAY` at least 15 minutes, and `AIRLINE_NAME` from the two-letter code, keeping the raw code if a name were missing, which never happened in this extract. `DEP_HOUR` is the scheduled departure hour clipped to 0–23, and `TIME_BLOCK` bins that hour as Overnight 0–5, Morning 6–11, Afternoon 12–16, Evening 17–20, and Night 21–23, so BQ3 and BQ5 can group by period without reading the raw clock field. Every row has a block, because scheduled departure is filled even when the flight never left. `DOW_NAME` maps `DAY_OF_WEEK` 1 through 7 to Monday through Sunday. The extract already used that numbering: 2015-01-01 was Thursday, and those 137 rows are 4, so we did not recode the integer, we only added the weekday name. `CANCEL_REASON_NAME` maps `CANCELLATION_REASON` A, B, C, and D to Carrier, Weather, NAS, and Security, and any other value, including blank, to Not cancelled, so we did not recode the letter, we only added the label. `SEASON` maps `MONTH` to DJF, MAM, JJA, and SON, December through February, March through May, June through August, and September through November, so grouping can use meteorological season without reading the month number.

No normalization was applied to any column. The stored `flights` table stays flight-level. `dashboard/metrics.py` aggregates on the filtered frame: delay rate, average delay, cancel rate, cause minutes, top origins. BQ5 is a second table, `clean/delay_risk_bands.parquet`, written by the same `clean.py` run. It is not a recast of the flight rows.

### b. Modeling / dashboard design

We structured the tables for a filterable BI app, not a fitted model. pandas writes `clean/flights.parquet` and `clean/delay_risk_bands.parquet`, `dashboard/metrics.py` holds the KPI functions, and Streamlit plus Plotly draw one `dashboard/app.py` with four tabs. Definitions are frozen in `KPI_DICTIONARY.md`. The page calls `flights`, `delay_rate`, `avg_delay_minutes`, and `cancel_rate` on the filtered frame instead of recomputing those ratios in the layout. Cause charts call `cause_minutes` and `cause_share`. Origin charts call `top_origins`.

The sidebar is shared by every tab. It has airline names and months 1 through 12, both defaulting to all. Clearing a list leaves zero rows, so the flights card shows 0 and delay rate, average delay, and cancel rate show a dash rather than 0% or 0 min. Reset is the only control that puts every airline and every month back. The flights frame is filtered once. Every tab uses that same `filtered` copy. Empty filter means empty. The app does not treat an empty list as "ignore this control."

Each tab opens with four cards. Flights is the filtered row count, including cancelled and diverted. Delay rate is delayed operated flights divided by operated flights, shown to one decimal percent. Avg delay minutes is the mean `ARRIVAL_DELAY` among delayed operated flights only. Cancel rate is cancelled rows divided by all rows in the filter. Delay-rate charts use the operated `DELAYED` mean, so the axis stays 0 to 1 with percent ticks and matches the delay-rate card. Cancelled and diverted rows stay out of delay-rate views.

Overview (BQ1) ranks airlines by delay rate, worst first. It draws delay rate by `MONTH` 1 through 12 with January on the left and a gap if a month is empty. It shows delay rate at the 15 busiest IATA origins by flight count from `top_origins`, bars in volume order. It ranks airlines by average delay minutes among delayed operated flights. Origin charts drop unmatched numeric airport IDs only.

Causes (BQ2) use delay minutes, not delay rate. The tab has a 100% stacked bar of cause share by airline and an overall pie of the five BTS cause columns Carrier, Weather, NAS, Security, and Late aircraft. Those minutes exist only for arrivals delayed at least 15 minutes.

Time & risk (BQ3, BQ5) shows delay rate by weekday from Monday through Sunday, delay rate by `TIME_BLOCK` from Overnight through Night, a weekday × time-block heatmap, and scheduled departure hour. All of those views use operated rows in the current filter. BQ5 does not rebuild that lookup in the app. It loads `delay_risk_bands.parquet` and shows the airline × weekday × time-block table. The airline filter applies to that table. The month filter does not, because the cells are year-round with a floor of 30 operated flights.

Cancels vs delay (BQ4) shows cancel rate by airline, a scatter of delay rate against cancel rate with point size equal to flights, and the same pair of views for the 15 busiest IATA origins. Cancel rate is cancelled ÷ all flights. Delay rate remains delayed ÷ operated. An optional stacked bar and pie show cancel-reason mix among cancelled flights only.

### c. AI/BI modeling

We did not train a classifier and we did not forecast 2016. `src/clean.py` writes a second table, `clean/delay_risk_bands.parquet`, as a historical BI lookup for BQ5. Operated rows only are grouped by `AIRLINE`, `DAY_OF_WEEK`, and `TIME_BLOCK`, each cell stores `n_flights` and `delay_rate` as the operated `DELAYED` mean, and cells with fewer than 30 operated flights are dropped, because a rate on a thin cell is noise. Remaining rates are banded Low below 15%, Medium from 15% up to but not including 25%, and High at 25% and above. We used those fixed cuts rather than terciles, so the same delay rate keeps the same band if the mix of cells changes. This extract kept 297 of a possible 490 airline–weekday–block cells, 99 Low, 114 Medium, and 84 High. `AIRLINE_NAME` and `DOW_NAME` are copied onto the lookup so a later page does not have to join the name maps again. The Time & risk tab loads that parquet. It does not recompute bands. The visual is the cell table, not a heatmap that collapses airline.
